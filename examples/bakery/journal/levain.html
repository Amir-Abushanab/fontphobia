Notes on levain

What a starter is, and why our bread takes 30 hours.

A levain is a pâte fermentée, a piece of old dough that keeps the wild yeast and bacteria alive from one bake to the next. Ours is fed twice a day, at 6am and 6pm, with equal weights of flour and water.

“Good bread is mostly waiting.”

The dough rests overnight, then comes the façonnage, the shaping, which is where a loaf gets its tension. A cold proof follows, and the bake itself is the shortest step: 45 minutes at 250 °C.

If you'd like to start your own, ask at the counter. We give away a spoonful of ours, with instructions, for free.
